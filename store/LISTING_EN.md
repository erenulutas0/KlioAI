# English store listing — KlioAI

Play's limits: app name 30 characters, short description 80, full description
4000. Everything below is inside them; the count is given after each field.

This is the **default** listing — the one Play falls back to for every device
whose language has no translation of its own. The Turkish listing names Turkish
because that is who reads it; this one says "your own language", which is what
the tutor does for a learner in any of the seven interface languages. The
Turkish sentence in the example stays: it is a real one, recorded on a device,
and it shows the mechanism better than a description of it.

Two rules. It sells one thing, and the rest is a paragraph. And it says its own
weakness — the AI sometimes mishears — because a learner who meets that in
the first conversation without having been told is a learner who leaves.

The mishearing paragraph is exact about when the app asks first, because the
earlier wording was not. It said the text is always shown before sending; on the
phone on 20 September a whole Turkish clause went straight through without a
confirm screen. That screen opens only when the transcription is unsure or a
second pass reads the clip as another language, and a Turkish sentence written
in plain ASCII letters ("biraz su olabilir miyiz") is neither. A listing must
not promise a step the first conversation skips.

The name and the short description were rewritten on 20 September for a reason the
numbers gave: 311 store impressions in 28 days, and a conversion rate of 19.44%.
The listing was converting the few people who reached it; almost nobody reached
it. Both fields are what Play ranks a search on, and neither carried a single
word anybody types. The hook moved into the short description's second half
rather than being lost.

## Full description (4000)

```
The moment every English learner knows: you are mid-sentence and the word is not there. KlioAI was built for that moment.

STUCK? SAY IT IN YOUR OWN LANGUAGE
In the spoken tutor you talk out loud to a character: a waiter, a hotel receptionist, a doctor, an interviewer. When you hit a word you do not have, say that part in your own language: "I'd like the pasta, and also biraz su alabilir miyiz?" Nothing stops. The waiter brings the water, and under your line a small card appears: what you said struck through, the English you needed beside it, and one line saying why. Forgetting a word stops being where the conversation ends and becomes where you learn it.

TRY IT WITHOUT AN ACCOUNT
The first time you open the app nothing is asked of you; you start talking. Sign in when you want to keep what you have done, and it moves to your account.

AN HONEST NOTE
You are talking to an AI, and it sometimes mishears. When it is unsure what it heard, it shows you the text first and you can correct it before it goes; when it is confident, it sends. So a wrong word does reach the conversation sometimes. It is not a replacement for a teacher; it is the courage to speak before you face one, or a stranger.

IN THE SAME APP
Whole public-domain books to read end to end (Sherlock Holmes, Aesop's Fables, Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde), tapping any word for its meaning inside that exact sentence, saved words that come back right before you would forget them, a short daily plan that actually ends, translation practice built from your own words, grammar guides with worked examples, and feedback on your writing.

The interface speaks seven languages, and the line under a correction that explains why arrives in yours. The tutor itself always speaks and answers in English; that is the part you are here to practise.

The word list, reviews, books and grammar guides are free. AI features, including the speaking tutor, run on a daily quota: new accounts get a 7-day trial quota, then a free daily allowance continues, and PRO lifts the ceiling. No ads, no streak that shames you, no leaderboard.
```
